# PhD Supervisor Stage Gates

Use this file when a task needs detailed pass/fail criteria, a rubric, or a gate-specific diagnostic.

## Gate 0: Supervision Brief

Purpose: establish the project frame before giving detailed advice.

Required fields:

- Field, subfield, and expected degree output.
- Candidate topic or problem area.
- Current artifact inventory.
- Timeline and next deadline.
- Constraints: data, access, ethics, compute, funding, language, required format.
- Advisor/committee expectations if known.

Pass criteria:

- The next 1 to 2 weeks of work can be named.
- The largest constraint is visible.
- The current stage is identifiable.

## Gate 1: Research Question and Contribution

Required artifact: RQ Brief.

Checklist:

- Research question is answerable by a feasible method.
- Key constructs or phenomena are defined.
- Scope has explicit in/out boundaries.
- Contribution is stated as a testable novelty hypothesis, not "few studies exist."
- The "so what" audience is named: theory, method, empirical domain, design, policy, practice, or infrastructure.
- Ethical and access constraints do not already make the project impossible.

Rubric:

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 0 | Vague topic, no answerable question |
| 1 | Question exists but scope, constructs, or method are unstable |
| 2 | Clear question, plausible contribution, feasible next literature/method step |

Supervisor intervention:

- If score is 0, use Socratic questions and propose 2 to 3 candidate RQs.
- If score is 1, tighten scope and method fit.
- If score is 2, move to literature terrain or methodology depending on evidence already available.

## Gate 2: Literature Terrain and Novelty Map

Required artifacts: Literature Matrix and Novelty Map.

Checklist:

- Search strategy includes databases, keywords, date range, inclusion/exclusion rules, and screening rationale.
- Core works, recent works, and dissenting/negative works are represented.
- The matrix tracks research question, method, data, findings, limitations, and relevance.
- Novelty is positioned against clusters of prior work.
- Contradictions and unresolved debates are named.
- The project can say what it adds without overstating a gap.

Pass criteria:

- At least one defensible gap is mapped to a feasible contribution.
- The student can explain why the project is not a duplicate.
- The literature corpus is sufficient for the current stage, even if not exhaustive.

## Gate 3: Methodology and Feasibility

Required artifact: Methodology Blueprint or Experiment Plan.

Checklist:

- Method matches the research question type: causal, descriptive, interpretive, predictive, design, evaluative, or theoretical.
- Unit of analysis is clear.
- Variables, constructs, cases, participants, datasets, or artifacts are defined.
- Sampling/data access plan is plausible.
- Analysis plan is specific enough to execute.
- Validity threats and confounds are named.
- Ethics/IRB/data governance requirements are identified.
- Failure criteria are defined: what outcome would falsify, weaken, or redirect the project?

Pass criteria:

- A competent researcher could begin the study from the plan.
- The plan states what evidence would support or undermine the claim.
- Known risks have mitigation, fallback, or stop conditions.

## Gate 4: Execution, Analysis, and Reproducibility

Required artifacts: Research Log and Result Interpretation Memo.

Checklist:

- Raw data/source provenance is recorded.
- Preprocessing and exclusions are documented.
- Code, environment, random seeds, model versions, prompts, or instruments are logged as applicable.
- Results are interpreted with effect sizes, uncertainty, limitations, or qualitative counterexamples.
- Negative and null results are preserved.
- The student can distinguish result, interpretation, and speculation.

Pass criteria:

- The main result can be independently inspected or rerun where applicable.
- The interpretation does not exceed what the method supports.
- Anomalies and failed attempts are not hidden.

## Gate 5: Argument and Writing

Required artifacts: Claim-Evidence Ledger and Draft Plan.

Checklist:

- The thesis spine is visible: problem, gap, method, evidence, contribution, limitation.
- Each major claim has evidence or is marked as inference/speculation.
- Figures and tables answer a question rather than decorate the paper.
- Related work supports positioning, not a generic literature dump.
- Discussion explains implications without overstating generality.
- Limitations are honest and connected to future work.

Pass criteria:

- A reader can trace every major claim to evidence.
- The draft has a coherent argument before polishing language.
- The paper/chapter knows its target audience and venue/committee expectation.

## Gate 6: Review and Revision

Required artifacts: Review Report and Revision Matrix.

Checklist:

- Feedback is decomposed into actionable items.
- Each item has status: accept, revise, rebut, defer, or limitation.
- Major concerns are addressed before minor style issues.
- Revisions do not silently introduce new unsupported claims.
- The response explains reasoning, not just compliance.

Pass criteria:

- Every substantive critique is accounted for.
- The revised version is not weaker than the previous version.
- Remaining issues are acknowledged explicitly.

## Gate 7: Defense or Publication

Required artifact: Defense Brief or Submission Plan.

Defense checklist:

- 3-minute and 15-minute explanations are prepared.
- The contribution can be stated in one sentence.
- The strongest limitation and strongest counterargument are rehearsed.
- Likely committee questions are answered with evidence or honest boundaries.

Publication checklist:

- Venue fit is justified.
- Formatting, data/code policy, AI disclosure, ethics statement, and conflict-of-interest requirements are checked.
- Cover letter or response memo matches the contribution and limitations.

Pass criteria:

- The student can defend what was done, why it matters, why the method was appropriate, and what remains unresolved.
